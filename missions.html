From Mission to Appointment

A framework for action

A mission becomes tangible through a qualified host, a defined project, a capable Fellow, and an accountable contribution.

  • Identify an institutional need of public consequence
  • Define the intended public benefit
  • Match the contribution to a Fellow’s field and capacity
  • Establish supervision, scope, and responsible practice
  • Document lessons and strengthen future work

How missions are chosen

Enduring need, credible leverage, public value

A Society mission is broad enough to matter across years and institutions, but it must also reveal practical places where individual expertise can make a responsible contribution.

Enduring consequence

The issue affects human dignity, shared knowledge, civic capacity, living systems, or the institutions on which communities depend.

Room for contribution

There are bounded questions, projects, research needs, operating challenges, or partnerships where a Fellow’s capabilities can be useful.

Potential to strengthen capacity

The work can leave behind knowledge, tools, relationships, methods, or institutional ability that extends beyond one intervention.

Working across missions

Complex needs rarely stay in one category

Public health may depend on civic trust. Climate resilience may depend on infrastructure, education, and public-interest technology. Scientific capacity may require policy, communication, and workforce development.

Mission categories provide orientation, not silos. An appointment may draw from several fields while remaining accountable to a primary purpose, a defined host, and a scope that can be managed responsibly.

This cross-mission view helps the Society assemble the right perspectives without losing clarity about who is responsible for what.
