Science Fellows · Robotics & Biotechnology R&D Track

Machines and living systems, advanced in service to people

The Robotics and Biotechnology R&D track is a named track inside the Science fellowship program. It is for Fellows whose research and engineering advance physical intelligence and programmable biology in a public-interest setting. This pathway is in development.

The mission

Research and development where robotics and biology meet

Science Fellows serve inquiry, discovery, communication, technology, and evidence-based service. The Robotics and Biotechnology R&D track applies that mission to two fields that are changing how discovery happens: physical intelligence, where robots learn, act safely, and work alongside people, and programmable biology, where laboratories run experiments on their own and biological production moves closer to the people it serves.

The track does not add a new program. It is a defined path inside Science Fellows, and the same standard of selection, appointment, and service applies. Work that leans toward building and deployment can also sit with Innovation Fellows, and work on resilience and critical systems with National Capacity Fellows.

Like every US Fellows appointment, it is not generic placement. Each appointment names a contribution of public value, not only a research output.

Two frontiers02

Physical intelligence and programmable biology, held to one fellowship standard of rigor, safety, and public benefit.

The host institution

Host-based, under the US Fellows appointment model

As with every US Fellows appointment, a Fellow on this track serves with a qualified host: a research institute, university, laboratory, or mission-driven organization doing robotics or biotechnology research and development. The host provides context, supervision, feedback, and a setting in which safety and research ethics are practiced with care. Host requirements and appointment terms remain separate from recognition as a Fellow.

The defined contribution

Four dimensions of the work

Each appointment names the contribution the Fellow will make and the benefit it should leave behind.

01

Physical intelligence

Advance robot learning, perception, and control, and human-robot teaming, so that capable systems serve many tasks and settings.

02

Assured autonomy

Develop the testing, verification, and human oversight that let people know when a machine can be trusted.

03

Programmable biology

Support self-driving laboratories, biomanufacturing, biosensing, and neurotechnology research that make discovery faster and more reproducible.

04

Workforce and public trust

Prepare the technicians, engineers, and scientists who follow, and explain benefits, limits, and risks plainly.

Responsible practice

Safety, ethics, and oversight come first

Work on this track follows the host's safety, biosafety, and research-ethics review, and any rule that applies to the specific project. Every appointment names its supervision, its scope, and its oversight obligations before the work begins, and a Fellow does not take on work the host has not cleared for them.

Results are reported with their uncertainty and limits. Where a project raises questions of safety, misuse, or public consequence, they are raised early and openly with the host. US Fellows promises no employment, pay, or funding through this track. Terms are confirmed at offer.

Who it is for

From interest to appointment

The track is for graduates, researchers, and engineers whose field and record meet what US Fellows' own criteria require, and whose work touches robotics, autonomy, biotechnology, or the systems around them. Selection is made by US Fellows. Expressing interest does not guarantee an appointment.

Researchers further along in their careers may also be recognized through the R&D Scholar designations, which complement the Society's field-based Fellow programs.

Express interest

Apply through the fellowship application, choosing Science Fellows, and describe your field, record, and the contribution you are ready to make.

Establish fit

US Fellows considers merit, readiness, mission alignment, and whether a qualified host can support the work responsibly.

Define the appointment

Confirm scope, host support, safety and ethics obligations, intended benefit, and a practical working cadence.

Serve and share

Complete the work with rigor, participate in Society life, and share what others can learn from it.

“Capable machines and programmable biology earn public trust through the people who build them with care.”

Take the next step
