The Fellowship Society

People joined by what they are willing to contribute

US Fellows is more than a program. The Society is the civic home of Fellows, advisors, alumni, Host Institutions, and mission partners.

It convenes people across disciplines and generations who share a commitment to public service, institutional strength, scientific progress, human dignity, and the future of civil society.

“Fellows serve missions, not ego. Fellows strengthen institutions, not merely themselves.”

Code of Service

The conduct expected of a US Fellow

Fellows are expected to act with integrity, humility, excellence, discipline, respect, and commitment to the public good.

Integrity

Be honest, responsible, and worthy of the trust placed in the fellowship.

Humility

Listen, learn, and place mission above personal recognition.

Excellence

Bring rigor and discipline to every contribution.

Respect

Honor the people, communities, and institutions affected by the work.

Stewardship

Leave institutions stronger and knowledge more useful.

Public Good

Judge success by the benefit created beyond oneself.

The Fellowship Oath

I accept the honor of fellowship in service to humanity, civil society, science, and the nation.

I will contribute my knowledge, discipline, imagination, and effort to missions larger than myself.

I will use my fellowship not merely as a distinction, but as a commitment.

A society that stays useful

Belonging continues after an appointment

The Society is designed to preserve relationships and public-purpose identity beyond one project. Its value grows when Fellows continue to exchange knowledge, mentor others, identify new missions, and help institutions learn.

Peer exchange

Fellows bring different disciplines and regional perspectives to difficult questions without expecting one field to supply every answer.

Institutional memory

Hosts, advisors, and alumni carry practical lessons forward so future appointments can begin with more context and stronger judgment.

Renewed service

Society participation creates routes to advise, convene, publish, mentor, or contribute again as needs and capabilities change.

Membership as practice: The Society is strongest when its name describes an active network of responsibility rather than a passive roster.
