Who the program serves

Do not apply as a transcript file. Apply as a fundable researcher.

The program serves international researchers and graduate-level candidates, often with a master's background, who want to move from interest and academic preparation into a funded doctoral research strategy, professor-led university research, and long-term R&D career options.

It is not generic admissions advising. Scholars learn to connect their academic background to real R&D funding ecosystems, to identify universities, faculty, and thesis directions that fit, and to build the evidence that helps a university understand why they belong in that research environment.

The program is equally useful to the people around a scholar: nominators, recommenders, families, universities, NGOs, and industry partners can read here what a scholar prepares and what US Fellows does and does not promise.

How it works

A structured research-to-career pathway

Each stage builds on the evidence from the one before it. A scholar moves forward when the work is ready, not on a fixed schedule, and every opportunity along the way is pursued under the rules of the university, sponsor, or partner that offers it.

The first six months

Build the foundation before you apply

The first six months are where the scholar works hard. The aim is to find the fundable research story before applying, and to arrive at January and other major PhD application deadlines with a credible packet instead of a transcript and a generic statement of purpose.

Participation stays voluntary and self-directed: the scholar sets the weekly pace and owns every decision. A six-month OKR and KPI dashboard keeps the sprint honest about what is finished and what is not.

Sprint work

  • Choose research themes and a thesis direction
  • Identify three to five strong university targets
  • Map faculty who could be strong advisor, PI, or key-personnel candidates under university rules
  • Scan funding opportunities from Grants.gov, SAM.gov, NSF, NASA, USDA, and similar sources
  • Build funding opportunity snapshots
  • Prepare a research concept note or proposal snapshot
  • Gather testimonials and recommendations
  • Assemble a credible application packet for major PhD deadlines
The 10-year journey

Not a six-month advising sprint: a long-range support system

The sprint is the beginning. US Fellows stays with the scholar as a support system through doctoral study and the first chapters of an R&D career, with the long-term aim of research leadership, grant and contract fluency, and a real choice of where to do the work.

Stages overlap and every path is different. Each stage involves eligible opportunities only, under the program boundaries.

  1. 0–1

    Years 0–1: readiness

    Research fit, funding scans, the application packet, and university applications.

  2. 1–5

    Years 1–5: doctoral study

    PhD coursework, assistantship responsibilities where offered, professor-led research, and dissertation development.

  3. 3–5

    Years 3–5: proposal and contract literacy

    Deeper proposal and contract-management literacy, partner exposure, publications, and conferences.

  4. 5–7

    Years 5–7: first research roles

  5. 7–10

    Years 7–10: R&D leadership

    Mature research leadership, grant and contract fluency, a developed partner network, and career choice.

Why funding literacy matters

Learn to speak both languages: university research and contract-aware R&D

Doctoral research is paid for by someone. Agencies such as NSF, NASA, and USDA publish what they want to fund, and federal grants and contracts appear on Grants.gov and SAM.gov. A scholar who can read those notices can see how a research question becomes a proposal, a grant, a contract, a university project, or a partner opportunity, and can show a professor that the thesis direction connects to real agency priorities.

Search broadly and verify narrowly: a search result is only a lead until its official notice is checked.

Grants.govSAM.gov

Check every opportunity

  • Research topic and agency priority
  • Submission deadline and anticipated award schedule
  • Applicant and citizenship eligibility
  • Required deliverables and data rights
  • Eligible lead organization, PI, and partner roles
  • Whether SBIR or STTR rules apply

Never describe a person, university, laboratory, company, or US Fellows as committed until authorized representatives have agreed in writing.

The four-part model

University + Professor + Funding + Partner

A strong doctoral research plan answers four questions at once. Scholars work on all four together, because each one makes the others more credible.

01

University

Three to five programs whose departments, laboratories, and funding practices fit the thesis direction.

02

Professor, advisor, PI

Faculty whose research matches. Under university rules a professor may lead sponsored work as principal investigator (PI) or name key personnel (KP); only the university and the professor decide those roles.

03

Funding lane

The agency programs, solicitations, and assistantship routes that could pay for the research, captured as funding opportunity snapshots.

04

Partner

NGO, industry, or federal contractor partners whose needs the research could serve, recorded only with their permission.

One packet that brings it together

A complete R&D Scholar application packet holds a scholar profile, the master's background, a proposed thesis direction, three to five target universities, five to ten faculty candidates, three to six funding opportunity snapshots, partner alignment, a recommendation plan, an application budget, and the next 30, 60, and 90 days.

After the PhD

Options a doctoral researcher may pursue

A completed dissertation opens several directions. Each is an opportunity a scholar may pursue where eligible, under the rules of the institution that offers it, and none is promised.

University postdoc

Continued research under a faculty mentor, often extending the dissertation.

University research role

A research position inside a university laboratory, center, or institute.

NGO research

Applied research with a nonprofit or public-interest organization.

Industry or federal contractor R&D

Research and development inside a company or contractor team.

A new funding lane

A deeper continuation of the thesis topic through a new proposal or sponsor.

R&D leadership

Over time, leading proposals, teams, and partnerships with grant and contract fluency.

Every role after the PhD depends on eligibility and on the rules summarized in the program boundaries.

Program boundaries

What US Fellows does and does not promise

  • US Fellows does not guarantee university admission.
  • US Fellows does not guarantee assistantships, grants, stipends, tuition waivers, or funding awards.
  • US Fellows does not guarantee professor participation, PI or KP roles, postdoc roles, OPT, employment, or immigration outcomes.
  • F-1 is a non-immigrant student classification.
  • Any work, OPT, STEM OPT, postdoc, internship, assistantship, or partner role must be authorized and must follow university, sponsor, and U.S. government rules.
  • The program is planning, mentorship, research readiness, and professional development support. It is not legal advice or immigration representation.
Outreach standard

Credibility before endorsement

The goal is not a generic letter, borrowed name, or implied institutional commitment. It is a credible network of people who understand the research question and can decide whether a specific, bounded contribution makes sense.

Begin with a concise thesis or research direction and, whenever possible, support from your advisor or professor at your home institution.

Ask for a defined contribution to a serious research effort—not approval in the abstract.

International Scholar outreach standard
Flexible international participation

Learn and contribute at a responsible pace

International R&D Scholar is a voluntary, part-time, and self-directed pathway for citizen scientists, students, independent researchers, engineers, technologists, and other motivated contributors located around the world. It has no fixed weekly commitment and can accommodate academic, professional, family, and community responsibilities.

Participation may include literature review, open-source research, scientific communication, data validation, technical writing, seminars, citizen-science activity, ethical technology assessment, collaborative discussion, and proposal support. Every assignment remains subject to applicable law, project controls, geography, data protection, export control, and the sensitivity of the work.

Choose the designation by the question

Public-policy research, procurement or contract analysis, and strategic-initiative research may be thematic directions here when each is anchored in a defined research question, evidence plan, or proposal. These examples describe subject matter for research; they do not describe an operational job.

Operational contract management, public administration, program delivery, and employment questions belong in host-based field fellowships or appointments, where a host defines the role and supervision. They are not part of the International R&D Scholar designation. The Scholar pathway remains voluntary, part-time, and self-directed and does not promise employment, compensation, visa sponsorship, clearance, or funding.

A credible network

Build the team deliberately

Not every project needs every role, and no person or organization should be listed without permission. A well-developed network may include:

Home advisor

An international professor or advisor who knows the thesis, degree context, and quality of the work.

Research scientist

A field-aligned scientist who may offer feedback, methods guidance, referral, or a defined proposal role.

U.S. higher-education collaborator

A professor or researcher who can evaluate fit with a laboratory, department, or eligible lead institution.

Industry or mission partner

A qualified technical lead or organization—such as a HUBZone firm—whose capability matches a real opportunity.

Before you write

Prepare one complete outreach package

A recipient should be able to understand the work, the evidence, the opportunity, and the exact request without a long exchange for missing basics.

  1. Thesis or project briefSummary, research question, objectives, methods direction, and intended public impact.
  2. Home-institution contextDegree, institution, advisor, and preferably an attached advisor endorsement or letter of support.
  3. Research recordCV or short bio, relevant publications, portfolio links, and previous work.
  4. Opportunity evidenceSeveral relevant solicitation or opportunity identifiers, each checked for topic, deadline, eligibility, deliverables, and lead-organization rules.
  5. Proposed networkExisting endorsements and collaborators, clearly distinguished from people who have not yet agreed.
  6. One specific askThe exact feedback, introduction, role, or decision you are requesting from this recipient.
Make the request answerable

Ask for one concrete form of help

01

Brief feedback on the thesis, research question, or methodology.

02

A subject-matter-expert, Key Personnel, PI, or Co-PI role for a named opportunity.

03

A referral to a more appropriate researcher, laboratory, program, or eligible lead.

04

Guidance on a specific solicitation, fellowship, assistantship, or sponsored-research route.

05

Permission to cite an endorsement or list an agreed role in a defined proposal or research plan.

06

A bounded meeting with an agenda and the documents needed for a useful decision.

Personalize every message. Explain why the recipient's expertise fits this work. Avoid mass endorsement requests and do not ask someone to infer what you need.

Evidence of participation

Build a record that another researcher can evaluate

A Scholar's record may include completed learning activities, research briefs, literature maps, technical writing, validated datasets, workshop participation, proposal contributions, endorsements, mentor feedback, and evidence of reliable, ethical collaboration.

Active standing may be reviewed according to participation, conduct, contribution, and continued mission alignment. A Scholar may move to inactive or alumni standing when availability changes.

No automatic advancement

International participation does not create employment, compensation, visa sponsorship, graduate admission, funding, clearance, access to restricted work, or entitlement to the Resident or any higher designation. A later application must satisfy that program's independent eligibility gate.

Professional safeguards

Protect people, institutions, and the work

Share responsibly

Do not send classified, export-controlled, proprietary, unpublished, personally sensitive, or otherwise restricted information through public forms or unsolicited email.

Represent permission accurately

Keep a record of endorsements and role approvals. Never imply supervision, employment, funding, proposal participation, lab access, or legal responsibility.

Respect the answer

A scientist or institution may decline, suggest another contact, limit feedback, or request a different channel. A thoughtful refusal is not a program failure.
